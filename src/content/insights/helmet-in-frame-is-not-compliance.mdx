Most PPE detection systems answer a simple question: *is there a helmet somewhere near this person?* Safety managers are asking a different one: *is this person wearing the right protection, correctly, for the job they are doing right now?*

The gap between those two questions is where pilots fail.

## 1. Presence is not wearing

A helmet carried in the hand, clipped to a belt or pushed back on the head is still "a helmet in the frame". A mask under the chin is still "a mask". Detectors trained on bounding boxes have no notion of *where on the body* an item sits.

**What we do instead:** estimate body keypoints for every tracked person and map each PPE item to the body region it must cover&nbsp;— helmet to the crown, goggles to the eyes, mask to nose and mouth, gloves to both hands. An item that is detected but in the wrong place is reported as *worn incorrectly*, not compliant.

## 2. Small items need their own pipeline

Gloves, safety glasses and ear protection occupy a few dozen pixels on a typical CCTV view, and they are often hidden by the worker's own body. Running one detector over the whole frame misses them.

**What we do instead:** detect and track the person first, take a high-resolution crop, and run per-item classifiers on that crop. Decisions are made over several seconds of a track, not a single frame&nbsp;— a glove hidden behind a box for half a second is not a violation.

## 3. The rule depends on the context

The PPE required at a grinding station is not the PPE required in the warehouse aisle, and hot work under a permit needs more again. A single global rule either floods the site with false alerts or misses what matters.

**What we do instead:** a PPE matrix of *zone × shift × task × permit*. Zones can be armed by machine state from the PLC, and tasks can be inferred from the scene&nbsp;— a welding arc means a face shield is required.

## 4. One violation, one event

A person without a helmet walking for ten seconds through a camera view can generate hundreds of frame-level detections. If each becomes an alert, supervisors stop reading them within a week.

**What we do instead:** one track with one violation becomes one event, with a start time, duration, severity and an evidence clip. Events escalate only if they persist, and every site has an alert budget we agree up front.
